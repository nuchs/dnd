# The Wolves' Den

## Summary

The heroes arrive at a town late at night and are directed to an inn. It has
been hard to find work and they are reaching the end of their supplies. They
meet the local law enforcement at the inn, who can offer work — or be guided
to them the next day.

They go through a series of tasks:
1. Clear out the local bullywog infestation in the sewers
2. Find some wolfsbane
3. Escort a package back from a nearby village. The package contains silver
   chains; they will be attacked by bandits
4. Obtain a ritual from a hag before the moon is full

The last three tasks are being done for a local wizard who has lycanthropy
(although he does not reveal this). The amulet he used to suppress the curse
was stolen, and he was urgently looking for a means to control the change
before the full moon.

He's already gone wild once and laid waste to the local bullywog village —
they fled and holed up in the sewers.

Once the emergency has passed, he sends the adventurers on their next quest:
to take back his amulet from the cult who took it.

## Party

This is for a party of four.

- **Player 1:** L2 Melee Druid (played by 11 year old)
- **Player 2:** L3 Assassin Rogue (played by 9 year old)
- **Player 3:** L3 Paladin (played by DM, there to absorb the hits)
- **Player 4:** L3 Bard / L3 Warlock (played by DM, the kids think he is just
  a bard — the warlock part is the big reveal later)

## Chapter 1. Arrival

The heroes have been travelling a long time without work, are reaching the
end of their rations, and have been caught out in a storm. With nowhere
obvious to camp, they've been pushing on, hoping to reach somewhere to stay
the night. They reach the town of **Garresh** a couple of hours before
midnight, soaked through and short-tempered.

### Scene 1. The Guards

**NPC — Corlen Ash, Gate Guard**
Human, late 40s, been on this gate long enough that a storm at midnight is
just another shift. Not suspicious by nature, just thorough — he's seen
enough bandits dressed as travellers to ask questions first. Not looking for
a fight and visibly relieved when he doesn't have to have one.

*Read-aloud / beats:*
- Lantern raised against the rain, halberd held more for the wet than the
  threat. "Bit late for honest folk. State your business."
- Asks names, where from, why Garresh, why tonight — gives each PC a
  moment to introduce themselves.
- Once satisfied (this should take almost no convincing — a DC 10
  Persuasion/Charisma check if anyone wants to speed it along, but by
  default just play it out), he relaxes.
- Directs them to **The Drowned Bell** for food and a bed, and mentions:
  *"If you're looking for coin, Captain Voss drinks there most nights.
  Always got some job or other going. Ask at the bar."*

This scene is a warm-up — no real obstacle, just a chance for introductions
and tone-setting before the inn.

### Scene 2. The Drowned Bell

The party arrives at a loud, packed inn — woodsmoke, wet dog, stew, the
noise of a town glad to be indoors tonight.

**NPC — Hesper Coln, Innkeeper**
Brisk, dry humour, unbothered by a room full of storm-refugees because
she's seen worse. Good source of throwaway gossip if asked — nothing that
needs to pay off, just texture. One optional line, if you want to plant
something for later chapters without spending it yet:
*"Strange howling out past the old mill, some nights. Dogs, probably."*

**NPC — Captain Reyna Voss, Head of the Town Guard**
Human woman, mid-40s, scarred knuckles, nursing a drink at the end of the
bar. She doesn't approach first — she watches. Practical, overworked, more
interested in results than heroics.

*Sequence:*
1. Food, lodging, general noise — let the party settle in.
2. Voss sizes them up before offering anything real. She floats two minor,
   slightly silly jobs first, mostly to see how they react:
   - Clearing giant rats out of the grain store.
   - Escorting the miller's daughter to market at dawn.
3. If the party seems capable (or just doesn't scoff), she gets serious:
   *"There's something bigger, if you're up for it. Bullywogs — moved into
   the sewers a few months back. Croaking through the grates at night,
   lights where there shouldn't be any. Lost a guardsman, Dobbs, checking
   a drain. Haven't seen him since."*
4. She's clear she'd rather they were driven off than slaughtered wholesale
   — cleaner outcome, fewer questions from the town council. Payment is
   modest but real (pick a number that suits your table — Garresh isn't
   rich).
5. **Seed for later:** if asked how long the bullywogs have been there, or
   where from: *"Showed up sudden-like. Driven out of somewhere upriver,
   by the state of them when they arrived."* Don't elaborate further —
   this pays off much later.
6. The party gets the following morning to shop (general store, a small
   apothecary — worth quietly having wolfsbane available for sale here if
   you want Chapter 3 to rhyme with this one, without drawing attention to
   it). Voss meets them after and walks them to the sewer entrance.

## Chapter 2. Spring Cleaning

> **Scaling note:** these encounters assume the L6 bard/warlock is playing
> support (buffs, utility, skills) rather than going all-in, per your setup.
> If he ever front-loads full damage, bump HP by ~15-20% on the combat
> encounters below to compensate.

### Session 1

#### Scene 1. Entry & Exploration

The party descends via a maintenance access or flood drain — Voss can point
them to whichever fits your map. Running water, old brickwork, the drip of
runoff finding its way down from the streets above.

- **DC 12 Perception/Survival:** bullywog tracks (webbed, dragging) head
  one direction; older bootprints (long dry, sewer workers) head another.
- Mention, but don't let them explore, 1-2 sealed routes — a collapsed
  tunnel marked on an old sewer plan as leading toward "the millhouse
  cistern." This costs nothing and does a lot of work making the place
  feel bigger than it is.

#### Scene 2. The Croc Pool

A flooded junction claimed by a large crocodile — not a bullywog, just an
apex predator that got there first. Territorial, not intelligent, no
connection to the main plot. Good early fight: everyone gets to
contribute, nobody's spotlight yet.

**Sewer Lurker (Crocodile, reflavoured)**
- AC 12, HP 21 (5d10 approx, +2 over standard for a sewer-fattened version)
- Speed 20 ft., swim 30 ft.
- Bite +4, reach 5 ft., 1d10+2 piercing; on hit can grapple (escape DC 12)
- Tactics: lurks submerged, attacks whoever gets closest to the water's
  edge, tries to drag a grappled target under

#### Scene 3. Minor Find

In the croc's midden, or on a drowned sewer worker's body nearby:
- A handful of gp
- A rusted but usable tool — grappling hook or crowbar, useful later
- A water-stained scrap of a hand-drawn sewer map showing far more
  tunnels than the party will ever set foot in (hand this over physically
  if you can — a prop does more work than a description)

#### Scene 4. The Flooded Grove — Druid Spotlight

A chamber gone strange: bioluminescent fungus on every surface, standing
water glowing faint blue-green. Something's blocking the only dry path
through.

**Sewer Leech (Giant Leech, reflavoured)**
- AC 12, HP 19
- Speed 5 ft., swim 30 ft.
- Bite +4, 1d8+2 piercing, and attaches (grapple, escape DC 12); while
  attached, deals automatic bite damage each round without needing to
  re-attack
- It's a beast, not a monster with a grudge — **Speak with Animals** lets
  the druid learn it just wants the party to stop coming near its nest,
  and it can be bribed off with food (a ration, a dead rat, whatever's
  handy) instead of fought. Entangle or clever terrain use (luring it into
  shallower water, blocking its retreat) also solves this without a
  straight fight.
- The fungus here is unusually lush and well-fed — plant the idea (DC 12
  Nature) that something is deliberately cultivating it further in. This
  is the transition zone into bullywog territory.

#### Scene 5. Edge of Bullywog Territory

No combat. Crude totems of bone and river-stone, croaking calls used as
markers, patches of fungus arranged with obvious intent rather than
growing wild.

- **DC 13 Investigation or Nature:** the arrangement isn't random — it
  reads as territorial marking, deliberate and recent, consistent with a
  settlement that's new to this ground rather than one that's lived here
  for generations.
- Good spot, if you want it, for a captured or overheard bullywog voice
  mentioning "the deep colony" or "the old cistern-keep" — places the
  party will never visit. Costs nothing, sells scale.

#### Scene 6. The Sentry Ledge — Rogue Spotlight

**NPC — Skreel, Sentry**
A young bullywog, more nervous than dangerous, perched on a pipe ledge
overlooking the only approach to the settlement proper. He's got a
warning horn on a cord round his neck and takes obvious pride in it —
also easily distracted (he's currently more interested in a shiny river
stone he's found than in actually watching).

**Skreel (Bullywog Sentry)**
- AC 15 (natural armour), HP 22
- Speed 20 ft., swim 40 ft.
- Bite +3, 1d4+1 piercing; Spear +3, 1d6+1 (melee or thrown, range 20/60)
- Standing Leap: long jump up to 20 ft., high jump up to 10 ft., with or
  without a running start
- If the horn sounds: reinforcements arrive in 1d4+1 rounds (have a small
  group of 2 bullywogs ready to go)

The rogue needs stealth and positioning to take him out before the horn
sounds — give a real route in (shadow along a pipe, a grate that bypasses
his sightline, a distraction the druid could set up from below). Fail
state is "alarm sounds, extra fight" — not a disaster, just a cost.

**End Session 1:** past Skreel's ledge, the tunnel opens out and the party
gets their first look down into the settlement proper — far bigger than a
few strays, cookfires, dozens of bullywogs, and in the distance, something
that's clearly a chief's seat. Good cliffhanger; end here.

### Session 2

#### Scene 7. Side Tunnel — Extra Exploration (optional, pacing buffer)

A side chamber, flooded to the ankles, with a minor find and — if you want
to start softening the party toward the bullywogs before the finale —
evidence of what actually happened to them:
- Crude jewellery, a cookpot, nothing valuable
- Torn cloth, unmistakably claw-marked, and a bullywog corpse with wounds
  that don't match anything the party's fought down here. This is the
  first physical hint of the loup garou attack, with nothing explained.

#### Scene 8. Approach Combat

A patrol — 2 standard bullywogs plus a shaman for variety — harder than
the croc, meant to raise tension before the chief's court.

**Old Muckwart (Bullywog Shaman)**
- AC 13, HP 27
- Speed 20 ft., swim 40 ft.
- Spear +3, 1d6+1 piercing
- Spellcasting (DC 11): 2/day *venom spit* (as Ray of Sickness, 2d8
  poison); 1/day *sludge wave* (as Create/Destroy Water, used offensively
  — knocks a line of targets prone, DC 11 Strength save); at will, once
  per short rest per ally, *shaman's croak* — reaction, grants an ally
  advantage on their next attack roll

**2x Bullywog** (as Skreel's stat block above, no horn)

Tactics: shaman hangs back and buffs/debuffs, bullywogs try to flank.

#### Scene 9. The Chief's Court

The cistern-keep — a wide, half-flooded chamber, old brick arches, a
raised dais where sewer water pools like a moat around a stone seat.

**NPC — Chief Bogsworth, the Marsh-Thane**
Old, heavyset, missing an eye. Tired in the way of someone who's already
lost more than he can afford to lose again. He doesn't want a war — not
because he can't win one, but because his people already ran from
something worse than this party and he's not willing to spend more lives
finding out if the heroes are as bad.

*Beats:*
- He already knows why they're here — Skreel's horn, or scouts, or
  simple common sense.
- He explains, plainly, that his village upriver was destroyed — something
  came through in the night, fast and savage, and killed without pattern
  or mercy. They fled here because stone and water were the only defence
  they knew. (This is the loup garou attack — the party may already
  suspect as much from Scene 7. Don't confirm more than the chief would
  actually know: he didn't see what did it, only what was left.)
- Rather than fight outright, he proposes **champion duels** — an old
  bullywog custom for settling disputes without bloodshed. Three
  champions, three challengers, submission not death. His word, once
  given, is binding on his people.

*(Bogsworth stat block, in case things go sideways — he does not fight
unless personally betrayed or attacked first)*
- AC 16 (natural armour), HP 45
- Slam +6, 2d6+3 bludgeoning
- Legendary among his own people rather than mechanically special — treat
  this as a last resort, not part of the intended encounter.

#### Scene 10. Finale — Champion Duels

A Pokémon-style sequence: three one-on-one bouts, non-lethal, submission
ends the fight. Suggested order:

1. **Rogue vs. Squirmander** — low stress, clean win, confidence builder
2. **Paladin vs. Charmasaur** — a real fight, the paladin goes down
3. **Druid vs. Bulbartle** — the save

**Squirmander**
- AC 16 (shell), HP 22, Speed 20 ft., swim 30 ft.
- Bite +4, 1d6+2 piercing
- Reaction — Withdraw: when hit, retreats into its shell for +4 AC until
  its next turn. Rewards patience over a straight damage race; bait the
  withdrawal, punish when it drops.

**Charmasaur**
- AC 14, HP 27, Speed 30 ft.
- Claw +5, 1d8+3 slashing, twice per turn
- Ember Burst (recharge 5-6): 2d6 fire in a small area — the thing that
  should tip the paladin below half HP and justify the loss
- Straightforward aggressive brute; should be close, not a stomp

**Bulbartle**
- AC 17 (natural armour), HP 30, Speed 20 ft.
- Slam +5, 1d10+3 bludgeoning
- Vine Snare (1/turn): target makes a save or is restrained — the obstacle
  the druid needs to solve with terrain or spells, not raw damage.
  Entangle, wild shape mobility, or just using the standing water should
  be what turns this fight, not good rolls alone.

**Non-lethal framing** ties directly back to the loup garou reveal: these
are people who just survived something genuinely terrifying and have no
appetite for more death. A downed champion yields; the chief's word is
binding. This also means the paladin "losing" costs nothing narratively —
it's expected, not a failure state.

If the dice go somewhere you didn't plan (rogue loses, etc.), let it land
— one lost duel doesn't break the arc and raises the stakes for whoever's
next. Keep a couple of soft levers in reserve rather than fudging rolls:
the chief calling a pause, a champion offering grudging respect and "one
more round," or a watching bullywog shouting unhelpful advice.

#### Epilogue

Heroes return to report results and collect their reward from Captain
Voss. Bullywogs begin relocating over the following days — visible from
town if anyone's watching the river. Whatever seeds you planted (the
howling past the mill, the claw-marked corpse, "somewhere upriver") stay
unresolved for now.
